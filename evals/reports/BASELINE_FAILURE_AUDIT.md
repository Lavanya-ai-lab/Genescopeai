The APOE gene encodes three isoforms—ε2, ε3, and ε4—differing at two amino acid positions: cysteine (Cys) to arginine (Arg) at position 112 and Cys to Arg at position 158 [pubmed_36348357]. The rs429358 single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) specifically refers to the C→T substitution at position 158 (rs429358: C/T), which results in the amino acid change from arginine (Arg) to cysteine (Cys) in the APOE protein. This change is critical: the ε4 isoform has Arg at both positions 112 and 158, while the ε2 isoform has Cys at both positions. Thus, the T allele at rs429358 corresponds to the ε2 allele (Cys158), and the C allele corresponds to the ε4 allele (Arg158) [pubmed_36348357]. The presence of the C allele at rs429358 (encoding Arg158) is the key nucleotide change associated with increased APOE ε4 risk, as it contributes to the structural instability of apoE4, promoting amyloid-β aggregation, impaired clearance, and neurotoxicity [pubmed_36348357]. While rs429358 is a proxy for the ε4 allele, the full risk is conferred by the combination of both rs429358 and rs7412 (C/T at position 112), which together define the ε4 haplotype [pubmed_36348357]. Therefore, the exact nucleotide change at rs429358 that explains APOE ε4 risk is the C→T transition, but the pathogenic effect is context-dependent on the presence of the Arg158 variant in combination with Arg112.
